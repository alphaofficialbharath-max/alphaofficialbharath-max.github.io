How to improve your typing speed: 7 habits that actually work

Most people who feel "slow" at typing do not need a better app - they need better habits. These seven changes took real people from under 30 words per minute past 50, and every one of them fits into a normal school day.

1. Chase accuracy before speed

Speed is earned, never typed by force. When you hammer keys at 60 WPM with 85% accuracy, you spend more seconds fixing mistakes than a calm typist at 40 WPM with 98% accuracy. Type at the maximum speed where you can stay clean, and your correct speed climbs by itself. The typing speed test on this site shows live accuracy for exactly this reason - watch that number, not the WPM counter.

2. Look at the screen, not your hands

Every glance down to the keyboard adds a small pause, and reading the wrong place in the sentence makes you retype from scratch. Trust the two bumps on F and J - they exist so your index fingers can find home base without looking. It feels slower for a week, then faster than you ever were while hunting for keys.

3. Type real sentences, not random letters

Your fingers learn patterns, not letters. Words like "through" and "there" repeat thousands of times in daily life, so practising on real sentences pays off inside your homework, chats and exam answers. This is why our test offers a quote mode: it trains the shapes you actually type on a normal day.

4. Keep a steady rhythm

Skilled typists add keys at an even beat, like a drum machine. Sprinting one word and stopping for the next is what makes your WPM bounce around wildly. When you notice lumps, slow down until the beat evens out - the plateau disappears and average speed rises.

5. Ten minutes daily beats two hours weekly

Typing is muscle memory, and muscle memory consolidates with sleep. A 10-15 minute daily round, maybe one 30-second sprint plus one minute-long run before you sleep, beats a weekend marathon your hands forget by Monday. Tie it to an existing habit - right after an evening class, for example - so you never negotiate with yourself.

6. Fix posture and hands

Feet flat, wrists lifted off the desk, elbows near your sides, shoulders down. Tension in your shoulders turns into mistakes minutes later, and wrists resting on the desk create the exact strained angle that long office typists complain about. A straight back and floating wrists cost nothing today and save years of discomfort.

7. Measure once a week

Take the same timed test each week - say every Sunday night - and write down WPM and accuracy. Progress you can see is the difference between quitting after ten days and training for a term. Most steady practitioners gain a comfortable 10-15 WPM in the first two months, and the gains keep arriving after that if you keep the routine.
